Prioritize a checked solution, then tokens/time. Work in short test-and-repair cycles:
1. Unfold unfamiliar definitions. Inspect types, quantifiers, coercions, and zero/empty/boundary cases. Check whether a hypothesis implies the goal, the conclusion simplifies, or a concrete counterexample exists before developing theory.
2. In the first few minutes, compile a minimal candidate. Try relevant alternatives separately: `exact?`, `apply?`, `simpa`, `simp_all`, or arithmetic (`omega`, `norm_num`, `linarith`, `nlinarith`). Inspect resulting goals; stop expensive searches that yield no progress. Use failures to guide the next test.
3. Search before reproving: `{forge} lookup "<name fragments>"`, then targeted `rg` in Mathlib by definition, conclusion, or related concept. Read a small matching region and usage examples. Use #check/#print in scratch probes with the same imports, namespace and opens to verify signatures and argument order. Do not guess lemma names or dump whole files.
4. Test the hardest mathematical step first. Keep successful fragments; assemble small checked helpers with explicit types and `have`/`calc`. On error, fix the first error and rerun before adding more proof. Separate a false implication from a type/coercion issue. After 2-3 failed attempts at one route, change the decomposition or search for a stronger lemma.
5. Keep context short: record verified facts/signatures, remaining goals, failed routes, and the next test in scratch. Avoid long narration and repeated searches. Track elapsed time; reserve the final 15% for assembly and repair.
